SECTION 1 — EQUIPMENT OVERVIEW
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1.1  Product Family Description

The CP-200 Series industrial reciprocating compressors are heavy-duty, single-stage
or two-stage air and gas compression units designed for continuous-duty industrial
applications. This manual covers the following models:

  CP-200   — Single-stage, 50 HP, 175 PSIG max, air-cooled
  CP-201   — Single-stage, 75 HP, 175 PSIG max, air-cooled with aftercooler
  CP-202   — Two-stage, 100 HP, 200 PSIG max, air-cooled with intercooler/aftercooler

All CP-200 series units share the same control platform (IW-CTRL-3000), identical
operator interface (4.3-inch color HMI), and the same error code architecture.
Refer to Section 7 for the complete error code reference.

The CP-200 family is engineered for environments requiring reliable, oil-flooded
reciprocating compression in manufacturing plants, refineries, food processing
facilities, and general industrial applications. Ambient operating temperature
range: 32°F to 115°F (0°C to 46°C). Altitude de-rating applies above 3,300 ft.

1.2  Major Component Assemblies

  1.2.1  Compression Assembly
         - Cast-iron cylinder block (bore: 5.5 in CP-200, 6.0 in CP-202)
         - Forged-steel connecting rods with replaceable bronze bushings
         - Hardened-steel crankshaft, dynamically balanced, journal-ground
         - PTFE-coated piston rings (service interval: 8,000 hours)
         - Stainless-steel reed valves (suction and discharge)

  1.2.2  Lubrication System
         - Pressure-feed splash lubrication with positive-displacement gear pump
         - Oil capacity: 3.5 quarts (CP-200/201), 5.0 quarts (CP-202)
         - Oil type: IW-CompOil 100 or SAE 30 non-detergent compressor oil
         - Oil pressure normal operating range: 18–35 PSI
         - Oil temperature normal range: 130–175°F

  1.2.3  Cooling System
         - Forced-air cooling via belt-driven fan (CP-200/201) or direct-drive fan (CP-202)
         - Aluminum fin-and-tube condenser coils (discharge air side)
         - Fin spacing: 12 fins-per-inch (susceptible to dust loading — see Section 5)
         - Cooling fan motor: 1.5 HP, 1,750 RPM (CP-200), 2.0 HP (CP-202)

  1.2.4  Control and Safety System
         - IW-CTRL-3000 microprocessor controller
         - Discharge temperature thermocouple (Type K, range: 0–400°F)
         - Discharge pressure transducer (0–250 PSIG)
         - Suction pressure transducer (0–50 PSIG)
         - Oil pressure switch (cutout at 15 PSI)
         - Motor thermal overload relay

1.3  Nameplate and Identification

Each unit carries a stainless-steel nameplate on the right side of the skid frame.
The nameplate includes: model number, serial number, rated HP, max working pressure,
electrical specifications, and manufacturing date. Serial numbers follow the format:
CP2-YYYYMM-XXXX (e.g., CP2-202203-0417 = March 2022, unit #417).

SECTION 2 — SAFETY
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1  General Safety Warnings

WARNING: Compressed air and gas systems contain stored energy that can cause
serious injury or death. All service personnel must be trained and authorized
before performing maintenance.

  - ALWAYS lock out / tag out (LOTO) the unit before performing any internal
    service. Refer to site LOTO procedure and OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147.
  - NEVER remove pressure-retaining components while the system is pressurized.
  - ALWAYS verify system pressure is ZERO before disconnecting piping.
  - DO NOT operate the compressor above its rated maximum discharge pressure.
  - ALWAYS allow the unit to cool for a minimum of 30 minutes after shutdown
    before touching discharge piping, cylinder heads, or valve covers.
  - High-voltage electrical hazards are present. Qualified electricians only
    for electrical service.

2.2  Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) Requirements

  - Safety glasses or face shield (minimum ANSI Z87.1)
  - Hearing protection (>85 dB — required within 3 feet of operating unit)
  - Safety-toed boots
  - Heat-resistant gloves for cylinder and discharge line service
  - Chemical-resistant gloves for refrigerant or oil handling

2.3  Emergency Shutdown Procedure

  In any emergency, press the RED "EMERGENCY STOP" button on the operator panel.
  This immediately de-energizes the motor starter and closes the inlet valve.
  Do not restart the unit without identifying and correcting the cause of the
  emergency shutdown. Log all emergency stops in the maintenance record.

SECTION 3 — INSTALLATION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1  Site Requirements

  - Minimum clearance: 36 inches on service side, 24 inches on all other sides
  - Floor load capacity: 2,500 lbs minimum (CP-200/201), 3,500 lbs (CP-202)
  - Mounting: grout-leveled concrete pad or vibration-isolated steel platform
  - Ventilation: minimum 20 air changes per hour; inlet air must be free of
    corrosive vapors, paint fumes, and combustible gases
  - Ambient temperature: 32–115°F; relative humidity: 10–90% non-condensing

3.2  Piping Connections

  - Discharge connection: 2-inch NPT male (CP-200/201), 2.5-inch NPT (CP-202)
  - Inlet connection: 3-inch NPT female with inlet filter/silencer
  - All piping must be independently supported — no piping loads on compressor
    connections exceeding 50 ft-lb bending moment or 200 lb force

3.3  Electrical Requirements

  - CP-200: 460V/3Ø/60Hz, 50 HP, 65A FLA, 90A MCA
  - CP-201: 460V/3Ø/60Hz, 75 HP, 96A FLA, 125A MCA
  - CP-202: 460V/3Ø/60Hz, 100 HP, 124A FLA, 160A MCA
  - Motor rotation: verify correct rotation before initial startup (see Section 4.1)

SECTION 4 — OPERATION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1  Pre-Startup Checks

Before initial startup or after extended shutdown, complete this checklist:

  [ ] Verify oil level is at FULL mark on sight glass (engine cold)
  [ ] Check that inlet filter is clean and properly seated
  [ ] Confirm all access panels and covers are secure
  [ ] Verify drive belt tension (deflection: 3/8 to 1/2 inch at midspan)
  [ ] Confirm system isolation valves are open
  [ ] Verify discharge pressure setting is correct for application
  [ ] Check motor rotation — looking at fan end, rotation must be CLOCKWISE
      (incorrect rotation will result in immediate E03 motor overload fault)
  [ ] Confirm all electrical connections are tight and grounded

4.2  Normal Start Sequence

  1. Power ON at main disconnect
  2. HMI will display: "READY — Press START to begin"
  3. Press green START button
  4. Unit starts in unloaded condition (inlet valve closes to 20% open)
  5. After 30-second warm-up, inlet valve opens to 100%
  6. Monitor discharge pressure rise to setpoint
  7. Verify discharge temperature remains below 175°F during first 30 minutes

4.3  Normal Operating Parameters

  Parameter                   Normal Range        Warning Threshold   Fault Threshold
  --------------------------  ------------------  ------------------  ---------------
  Discharge Pressure (PSIG)   100–175             >170                >185
  Discharge Temperature (°F)  140–175             >175                >185
  Suction Pressure (PSIG)     0.5–8               <1.0                <0.3
  Oil Pressure (PSI)          18–35               <20                 <15
  Motor Amps                  ≤FLA rating         >90% FLA            >110% FLA
  Vibration (in/sec)          <0.15               >0.25               >0.40

4.4  Normal Shutdown Sequence

  1. Press STOP on HMI
  2. Unit enters unloaded coast-down mode (30 seconds)
  3. Motor de-energizes; inlet valve closes
  4. Discharge check valve prevents backflow
  5. Allow unit to cool 15 minutes before touching hot surfaces

SECTION 5 — MAINTENANCE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.1  Preventive Maintenance Schedule

CAUTION: Failure to follow the preventive maintenance schedule will void the
equipment warranty and may result in equipment failure, property damage, or
personnel injury.

  Interval          Task
  ----------------  ---------------------------------------------------------------
  Every 8 hours     Check oil level on sight glass; check for unusual noise/vibration
  Every 100 hours   Belt inspection: check tension, condition, and alignment (5.2)
  Every 250 hours   Air filter replacement (5.3); inspect valve reed condition
  Every 500 hours   Engine oil change (5.4); oil filter replacement
  Every 1,000 hrs   Full valve inspection and regapping; belt replacement
  Every 2,000 hrs   Major overhaul: piston rings, rod bearings, crank bearings
  Every 6 months    Condenser coil cleaning (5.5) — UPDATED per Bulletin 2024-001
  Annually          Thermocouple calibration; pressure transducer verification

NOTE: Bulletin BULL-2024-001 (effective 2024-01-15) updated condenser cleaning
frequency from annually to every 6 months for units operating in dusty or
industrial environments. This is a critical update to prevent E17 High Discharge
Temperature faults caused by condenser fouling.

5.2  Belt Inspection and Replacement (Every 100 Hours)

  Required Tools: Tension gauge (IW-TG-100), straight edge, torque wrench

  1. LOTO the unit (see Section 2)
  2. Remove belt guard (4 × 3/8-in hex bolts)
  3. Check belt tension: press firmly at mid-span; deflection should be 3/8–1/2 in
     under 10 lb force. If belt deflects more than 1/2 in, tighten via motor
     adjustment slots. If less than 3/8 in, loosen.
  4. Inspect belt for: cracking, glazing, fraying, or embedded debris
  5. Check pulley alignment with straight edge across both pulley faces (max
     misalignment: 1/16 in over 12 in span)
  6. Replace belt if cracks, glazing, or worn edges are observed
  7. Replacement belt: IW-BELT-CP2-A58 (CP-200/201), IW-BELT-CP2-B68 (CP-202)
  8. After replacement, re-tension and re-check after 8 hours of operation

5.3  Air Inlet Filter Replacement (Every 250 Hours)

  Filter element: IW-FILT-CP2-A01
  1. LOTO the unit
  2. Unscrew filter housing cap (hand-tight only)
  3. Remove and discard old element
  4. Wipe housing interior with clean dry cloth
  5. Install new element; ensure gasket seats evenly
  6. Reinstall cap — hand-tight plus 1/4 turn only (over-tightening crushes gasket)
  7. Record replacement in maintenance log with date and unit hours

5.4  Oil Change Procedure (Every 500 Hours)

  Oil type: IW-CompOil 100 (preferred) or SAE 30 non-detergent compressor oil
  Capacity: 3.5 quarts (CP-200/201) | 5.0 quarts (CP-202)

  1. Run unit for 5 minutes to warm oil to operating temperature
  2. LOTO the unit
  3. Place drain pan beneath oil drain plug (3/4-in NPT, bottom of crankcase)
  4. Remove drain plug; allow oil to drain completely (~10 minutes)
  5. Remove oil filter (IW-OILF-CP2-01); discard old filter
  6. Install new oil filter; pre-fill with clean oil before installing
  7. Reinstall drain plug with new crush washer; torque to 35 ft-lb
  8. Fill crankcase to FULL mark on sight glass
  9. Run unit for 2 minutes; check for leaks; recheck oil level
  10. Record oil change in maintenance log with oil type, date, and unit hours

5.5  Condenser Coil Cleaning (Every 6 Months — Per Bulletin BULL-2024-001)

  IMPORTANT: Fouled condenser coils are the most common cause of E17 High
  Discharge Temperature fault on CP-200 series units. Per Bulletin BULL-2024-001,
  cleaning interval was reduced from annually to every 6 months for industrial
  environments. If the unit operates in environments with airborne dust, fiber,
  or debris, monthly visual inspection is recommended.

  1. LOTO the unit
  2. Use compressed air (max 100 PSI) to blow through fins in reverse-flow direction
     (i.e., from discharge side to inlet side)
  3. If more than 1/8-inch of debris buildup is visible, use IW-COIL-CLEAN spray;
     allow 10-minute dwell time; rinse with low-pressure water; allow to dry
  4. Inspect fins for bending or damage; straighten with fin comb if needed
  5. Verify cooling fan blade condition — no cracks, bending, or debris
  6. After coil cleaning, run unit and monitor discharge temperature to confirm
     improvement (should drop 10–20°F if fouling was severe)
  7. Document cleaning in maintenance log; photograph coil before and after

SECTION 6 — TROUBLESHOOTING
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

6.1  Troubleshooting Methodology

When a fault occurs, follow this diagnostic sequence:
  1. Note the error code displayed on HMI
  2. Record operating conditions at time of fault (pressure, temperature, amps)
  3. Allow unit to cool if temperature-related fault
  4. Do NOT clear fault and restart without identifying the root cause
  5. Use Section 7 (Error Codes) for specific fault diagnosis
  6. Correct the root cause before restarting
  7. Monitor unit for 10–15 minutes after restart to confirm resolution

6.2  High Discharge Temperature — Diagnostic Tree (E17)

When E17 fires, the discharge temperature has exceeded 185°F. This is a
thermal protection shutdown to prevent cylinder head damage, valve failure,
or oil coking.

CRITICAL NOTE: E17 on CP-200 series = HIGH DISCHARGE TEMPERATURE.
This is a THERMAL fault — NOT an electrical or frequency fault.
(Note: CP-300 series uses a completely different error code architecture;
do not cross-reference CP-300 documentation for CP-200 E17 diagnosis.)

Probable causes (in order of frequency):
  A. Fouled condenser coils (most common — >60% of field cases)
  B. Cooling fan failure (belt broken/slipped, fan motor failed)
  C. Low refrigerant charge (if unit uses refrigerant cooling circuit)
  D. Blocked discharge piping causing excessive re-compression
  E. Ambient temperature above 115°F
  F. Faulty discharge temperature thermocouple (check calibration)
  G. Defective discharge temperature sensor wiring

Diagnostic steps:
  Step 1: Verify condenser coils are clean (see 5.5). If >1/8 in fouling, clean coils
  Step 2: Check cooling fan — confirm belt intact, correct tension, correct rotation
  Step 3: Verify cooling fan motor operation (measure voltage at motor terminals)
  Step 4: Check coolant level if applicable (CP-202 with optional water aftercooler)
  Step 5: Measure actual discharge temp with calibrated thermometer; compare to
          HMI reading. If >5°F discrepancy, suspect sensor fault
  Step 6: Verify ambient temperature is within specification (<115°F)
  Step 7: Check discharge piping for blockage or closed valves

6.3  Common Faults and Field-Expedient Checks

  Fault             Quick Check                              Typical Fix
  ----------------  ---------------------------------------  -----------------------
  E03 Motor OVL     Measure motor amps (all 3 phases)        Check rotation, unload
  E08 Low Suction   Check inlet filter; listen for hissing   Replace filter or repair leak
  E12 Oil Pressure  Check oil level on sight glass           Add oil or replace pump
  E17 High Temp     Check condenser coils and cooling fan    Clean coils; fix fan
  E21 High Press    Check discharge piping for blockage      Open isolation valve

SECTION 7 — ERROR CODES
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

7.1  Error Code Architecture

The IW-CTRL-3000 controller uses a two-character alphanumeric fault code system.
Error codes E01–E25 are active-fault codes that cause shutdown. Warning codes
W01–W10 are advisory — the unit continues to run but logs the condition.

IMPORTANT MODEL NOTE:
Error codes on CP-200 series are specific to this product family. Error codes
on CP-300 series use a different definition set for several shared code numbers.
ALWAYS confirm the model before referencing error code documentation.
For CP-200 series: refer only to document CP200-ERR-001 or this section.
For CP-300 series: refer only to document CP300-ERR-001.

7.3  Detailed Fault Description: E17 — HIGH DISCHARGE TEMPERATURE

Fault Code:        E17
Fault Name:        HIGH_DISCHARGE_TEMPERATURE
Model Applicability: CP-200, CP-201, CP-202 (ALL CP-200 SERIES)
Fault Category:    Thermal Protection

Trigger Condition:
  The Type-K thermocouple mounted on the discharge flange measures a temperature
  greater than 185°F (85°C). The controller applies a 2-second filter to prevent
  nuisance trips from momentary spikes. If the temperature remains above 185°F
  for more than 2 continuous seconds, E17 is latched and the unit shuts down.

WARNING LEVEL:
  A warning (W-HTEMP) is activated at 175°F. This allows operators 5–10 minutes
  to investigate before a fault shutdown occurs. Heed all W-HTEMP warnings
  immediately to prevent unplanned shutdowns.

Root Causes:
  PRIMARY — Fouled condenser coils (accounts for >60% of field E17 incidents)
    The CP-200's air-cooled condenser coils accumulate dust, lint, and industrial
    debris. Blocked fins restrict airflow, reducing heat rejection capacity.
    In heavy industrial environments, coils may foul in as little as 60 days.

  SECONDARY — Cooling fan system failure
    A broken drive belt will disable the cooling fan, causing immediate thermal
    runaway. Check belt condition first if coils are clean. Also check fan blade
    condition — a cracked or bent blade reduces airflow by up to 40%.

  TERTIARY — Insufficient coolant (CP-202 with water aftercooler option)
    If the unit is equipped with a water-cooled aftercooler, low cooling water
    flow will cause elevated discharge temperature. Minimum flow rate: 15 GPM.
    Verify supply valve is fully open and cooling water temperature is <85°F.

  OTHER — High ambient temperature, blocked discharge piping, refrigerant shortage,
    defective thermocouple, wiring fault at TC connection (see Section 6.2)

Immediate Action Required:
  1. STOP COMPRESSOR — do not continue operation above 185°F discharge temperature
  2. Allow unit to cool for minimum 15 minutes before inspection
  3. Check condenser coils for fouling (Section 5.5)
  4. Check cooling fan belt condition and tension
  5. Verify cooling fan is rotating at correct speed and direction
  6. If cooling water circuit present, verify flow rate (min 15 GPM)
  7. Correct the identified root cause before restart

Restart Procedure after E17:
  1. Confirm all E17 root causes have been identified and corrected
  2. Verify discharge temperature has cooled to <120°F on HMI display
  3. Press RESET on HMI panel (or momentary button on door panel)
  4. Fault clears; HMI returns to "READY" state
  5. Press START; allow unit to run in unloaded condition for 5 minutes
  6. Monitor discharge temperature — should stabilize below 165°F within 10 minutes
  7. If discharge temperature climbs again toward 175°F, STOP unit and re-investigate
  8. Document fault, root cause, corrective action, and restart in maintenance log

Associated Warning:
  W-HTEMP triggers at 175°F. If W-HTEMP appears, do not dismiss and continue
  running — investigate immediately using the E17 diagnostic tree (Section 6.2).

7.4  Detailed Fault Description: E08 — LOW SUCTION PRESSURE

Fault Code:        E08
Fault Name:        LOW_SUCTION_PRESSURE
Trigger Condition: Suction pressure <0.3 PSIG for >5 seconds
Root Causes:
  - Severely clogged inlet air filter (most common)
  - Refrigerant leak in suction piping (if process gas application)
  - Closed or partially closed inlet isolation valve
  - Inlet piping obstruction
Immediate Action:
  1. Stop compressor
  2. Inspect inlet filter — replace if visually clogged (Section 5.3)
  3. Check inlet isolation valve is fully open
  4. Inspect suction piping for obstruction, crimping, or collapsed hose
  5. Leak-test suction piping if filter is clean
Restart: Clear fault; restart; verify suction pressure rises to normal range (0.5–8 PSI)

7.5  Detailed Fault Description: E03 — MOTOR OVERLOAD

Fault Code:        E03
Fault Name:        MOTOR_OVERLOAD
Trigger Condition: Motor current exceeds thermal overload relay setting for >10 sec
Root Causes:
  - Incorrect motor rotation direction (reversed phase connection)
  - Mechanical binding in compression assembly
  - Worn piston rings causing excessive friction
  - Overloaded discharge pressure (back-pressure higher than rating)
  - Low supply voltage causing high current draw
  - Faulty motor windings
Immediate Action:
  1. LOTO unit
  2. Measure motor winding resistance (all 3 phases should be within 5% of each other)
  3. Verify phase-to-phase voltage at motor terminals (should be 460V ±5%)
  4. Check motor rotation marking on motor fan cover (must be CW from fan end)
  5. Attempt to rotate compressor shaft by hand (should turn freely without binding)
  6. Check overload relay trip class and setting — verify against motor FLA nameplate
Restart: Reset overload relay; address root cause; restart and monitor amps on HMI

7.6  Detailed Fault Description: E12 — LOW OIL PRESSURE

Fault Code:        E12
Fault Name:        LOW_OIL_PRESSURE
Trigger Condition: Oil pressure switch opens (below 15 PSI) for >3 continuous seconds
Root Causes:
  - Low oil level (check sight glass before all else)
  - Worn or failed oil pump gear set
  - Blocked oil filter
  - Oil pressure switch failure
  - Crankcase diluted with condensate (milky oil on dipstick — change oil immediately)
Immediate Action:
  1. STOP unit immediately; continued operation without oil pressure causes
     catastrophic bearing failure within seconds
  2. Check oil level on sight glass (cold reading; add oil if below FULL mark)
  3. If oil level is correct, suspect pump or switch failure
  4. Do NOT restart until oil pressure is confirmed above 18 PSI on startup
Restart: After correcting cause, restart and immediately check oil pressure reading on HMI;
  if pressure does not reach 18 PSI within 10 seconds, STOP unit; do not continue
